    Pin roll

    Tap a cell to add or remove a pin; drag to scroll. With a scale chosen, only its notes are offered (numbers are scale degrees; the root is gold). Each row is one tine of the comb; the playhead is the cylinder's real angle, so you can edit while it plays.

    About Frisky

    Frisky is a music box that isn't a recording. Nothing you hear is sampled. Every sound comes from physics worked out as it plays: steel tines plucked by pins on a turning cylinder, the comb ringing through its bedplate into the box, the box into the table, the table into the room, and the room into your two ears.

    It's named for Frisky, a golden hamster from a boyhood long ago. He lives here again, in a glass tank beside the box: he sleeps through the day, eats, stuffs his cheek pouches, digs and runs at night by your own clock. Belt his wheel to the crank and his pace becomes the tempo.

    What's simulated

    • The tines: each one a tapered steel beam with its own modes, tuned by length, thickness and lead weights, plucked and damped by real contact with the pins.
    • The drive: your hand on the crank through a ratchet, or a wound spring held to time by a fan governor, or the hamster's wheel through an O-ring belt.
    • The hamster: a 125 g body on a wheel, with leg forces, gaits, stride rates, heart rate and metabolism taken from studies of golden hamsters, and a day of behaviours from hamster ethology.
    • The room: walls, floor and furniture as reflecting surfaces, an Alpine hut or five other rooms, each with its own reverberation (and its own clock, showing the real time).
    • Your ears: a head-related transfer function on every sound path, so with headphones the box sits where you see it. Walk around, crouch, lean over the table and listen.

    How to play

    Light the candle. Circle the dial to crank, or wind the key and pull the lever, or let the hamster run. Choose a tune, or compose your own on the pin roll. Walk with W A S D, look with the arrows, and double-tap the box to lean in.
